Preoccupation Update

why this blog exists

 24 May '26

My (decade long now) preoccupation with the human-tech relationship can be summarised as: Navigating the ethics of human-technology interface design.

Moral responsibility and ethics cannot be separated from technical work, nor can they be added downstream.

Some builders are too inside the field to ask the foundational questions, and separate themselves from accountability. The lag between production and impact is too great for policy, law or academics to intervene quick enough. The consequences of these assumptions are held by those who do not have a say in deployment.

The questions the interface forces us to ask:

  • What are the impacts to human psychology and society when it interfaces with, or is mediated by, technology?
  • What are the quiet, structural gaps that occur when we design new technology or transpose old systems and mental models to new?
  • How can we mitigate failures at scale, especially when failure modes can be emergent in complex systems?
  • Who is responsible for the moral gap between designers/builders of new technology and what the actual impacts are? This is a critical risk as builders may not align to the best interests of their users.

This is why we need roles who work with both rigour and academia, enough to embed safety in their designs i.e. institution-minded builders, and an intersection of psychology, ethics, design, AI governance i.e. "Technolosophy".

How this has shown up for me so far:

  • Topics
    • physical (architecture)
    • psychological and emotional (clinical psychology, depth psychology)
    • digital (marketing, design, information architecture)
    • political and legal (uses of AI in the military)
  • Technolosophy
    • Technology ethics or philosophy of technology - what does tech do to us and our society? Values, social arrangements, sense of self. Is it something we chose or something that happened to us?
    • Critical design and design ethics - what values are embedded in designed objects and systems, who designed them and for whose benefit, what moral responsibilities do designers carry.
    • Moral psychology of technology - how does technology shape moral perception, moral reasoning, moral behaviour? What does sustained use of a product do to how a person relates to grief?
    • Applied AI ethics - practitioner - gap between AI ethics as academic and policy discipline

Practically speaking: Conversation design as an avenue to apply AI ethics at the institution layer.

And why this blog exists.
