Are AIs really Thinking Partners?

LLMs as a 'second brain'

 25 May '26

I came across Karpathy's concept of "a second brain" a month ago and since then my feed has been full of people exploring how to integrate LLMs into Obsidian.

In brief, LLMs can be used to summarise inputs (such as research topics or personal notes) to create a large graph of information. You can then use an LLM to query, question and investigate your understanding. These questions will form a loop that feed back into the knowledge base and add to it, such that you accumulate personalised knowledge.

But I wondered if this approach is actually undermining the "knowledge" or "learning" that's foundational to knowledge work.

The "Flattening Effect"

AIs are marvellous at quickly finding the through line of a text. However, if AIs are being used to summarise, the knowledge base that is being built is not actually being personalised. Several people can read the same source but have completely different takeaways, interpretations or highlights.

The substitution is concerning if you subscribe to Sonke Ahrens' paradigm of learning. The belief is that the act of summarising and wrestling with ideas IS the whole point of learning. The friction is crucial to generate original ideas as you understand the text through your own lens developed from past material you've read and experiences you've been exposed to.

If someone does not interact with material directly, they will not be able to surface the particular points they found intriguing. Pushing the frontier of human knowledge forward relies on different people pursuing their point of intrigue for long enough. (See Matt Might's "Put a dent in human knowledge" (https://matt.might.net/articles/phd-school-in-pictures/))

Accelerationist "learning"

There is a difference between learning in terms of "wisdom" and "trivia accumulation".

I can imagine that this encourages quantity over quality, particularly if someone is optimising for a seemingly large graph with thousands upon thousands of nodes. Using an LLM to summarise sources can accelerate the process of creating quick summary bites from hundreds of sources without a criterion or standard of credibility. Is the user actually asking whether the source should be used at all?

It reminds me of Dwarkesh's question to Michael Nielsen about "how to actually learn?". My interpretation of his question was "how do you actually accumulate wisdom?" because his process was purely speaking to many different experts in their field but not actually sitting down to reflect on what he had learned from each.

Personalisation

Even if questions are asked retroactively, is that enough to personalise the knowledge base? I would say no since your starting point of knowledge has already been artificially pruned based on what the LLM understands as the 'best average' or 'generally important' to everyone.

Solution

Sonke Ahrens' original solution to knowledge work still holds in these situations.

The slip box provides the opposite of breadth but provides the depth of your own mind, your specific reactions, your idiosyncratic "intellectual taste".

A middle ground

While I disagree with Karpathy's approach of LLM-assisted knowledge graphs if LLMs are being used to substitute the learning stage, I can see it working in the context of understanding technical or foundational knowledge. Fundamental topics like mathematics or physics are less in flux than topics like aesthetics or design i.e. concepts that have an angle of subjectivity.

Therefore, I feel as though the value of an LLM-assisted knowledge graph depends on what stage in the human's thinking process is employed, the context, and the type of information that the graph is being fed.

Thinking further ahead, if everyone's knowledge bases follow the same process, uses the same formula, has the same "best average" answer, would that create echo chambers of knowledge? If knowledge does not have conscious decisions in terms of input, the problem will (exponentially) compound at scale over millions of knowledge workers. And in this modern age, everyone is handling information in some shape or form. It would be a dangerous end state for all of us.
