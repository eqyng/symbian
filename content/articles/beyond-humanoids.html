Beyond Humanoids

What happens after robots?

 26 Sep '26

Around this time last year, I came across an article: https://www.dw.com/en/worlds-first-humanoid-robot-games-begin-in-china/a-73652714

"World's first humanoid robot games".

I was working with voice agents then, and was about to begin a project in the IoT space (and where I first heard the term "digital twin" in an architectural sense).

I was thus convinced that the next logical step after agents would be a rapid expansion into robots. How else could this new technology become more integrated into our lives if not in a sensory way? We were already developing a (deep and emotional) relationship to it, how could we make it even more 'real' to us? It made sense that the progression would move from text to speech to physical space.

We've spent the last few decades trying to enter a computer's world with the development of programming languages. Now we are asking it to adapt to ours by making natural language and fine motor skills the standard modes of operation.

Now Physical AI and Ambient AI are dominating the headlines - encompassing both ends of the intelligence spectrum from embodied to disembodied. Not necessarily surprising given all the developments that are happening in parallel, at pace and geopolitically. Take, for instance, a non-exhaustive list that includes the US-China AI commercial race, Ukraine-Russia war advancing AI drone technology, and Singapore's prototype data centre powered by brain cells.

But this has led me to question my previous assumption that robots were the endgame. Now that we've seen the black and white ends of the intelligence spectrum, it makes sense that we'll work backwards from there, moving into the grey spaces and gradations. What happens if physical ai was not necessarily the final stage?

Perhaps too science-fiction and speculative, but I believe science-fiction's value is that it provides a cultural landing point and possible end state for all the experimentation we're seeing today. It's almost a way for us to culturally recognise these patterns when and if the products eventually arrive (or even subconsciously build towards). Whether or not these ends eventuate, a part of us will be able to sense familiarity when we see it.

Two ideas I have are: the interface becomes human and intelligence becomes portable.

Axis 1: Brain-Computer Interface (BCI)

I went to a public seminar years ago, and a presenter held up his left hand to the audience. We could see a small scar below the webbing between thumb and forefinger. "A chip for convenience," he declared excitedly. They were offering participants a similarly embedded chip for $100. This was my first introduction to the possibility of interfaces becoming more intimate, stepping into the gradations of grey space.

The ultimate convenience is anticipatory. And what better way to anticipate someone's needs before they even know it themselves?

Two companies come to mind as examples of the human-side interface - Neuralink and Q.ai.

Neuralink's Telepathy product bypasses damaged neural pathways to actualise thoughts into robotic limb direction. Q.ai on the other hand relies on its 'whisper technology' of mouthed or spoken words ("facial skin micromovements") and other biometric indicators like heart rate and respiration rate.

Not scientific in the slightest but the game Cyberpunk does come up in my thoughts quite often as the end of line in terms of cyborgs.

Axis 2: Substrate-agnostic Intelligence

In terms of the device-side interface, science-fiction has already given us a few thoughts. For example, Iron Man's JARVIS and Ultron could move between various environments like houses or robots which were instantiations of a centralised intelligence.

With current technology, home lab hobbyists are already creating prototype JARVIS' that integrate smart devices like lights, music, and calendars to build an administrative spine for daily life. We're also already seeing enterprise projects like Google's recent update on cross-device AI memory which aims to provide cloud scalability while remaining as secure as local devices: https://deepmind.google/blog/advancing-private-ai-compute-with-secure-server-side-memory/.

A "substrate-agnostic intelligence" is similarly fluid and has different manifestations across diverse environments both software and hardware. But it is its own independent entity, not merely as a spoke of a hub.

Unlike today's technology, that uses the same affordances (i.e. opening your profile on your phone or computer opens a window into a single server), a substrate-agnostic intelligence adapts between environments of different affordances - a computer, robotic arm, smart glasses, or even more extreme, BCI as demonstrated in Axis 1. Another popular cultural example is I,Robot's VIKI who could appear as the same entity in different environments - in autonomous vehicles, houses and robots.

If the last example was possible, we would arguably reach the true middle of Axis 1 and 2 - a convergence of seemingly different technologies. To make an inductive comparison, a similarly historical pattern is how mobile computing and the smartphone itself emerged from a combination of separate technologies (battery miniaturisation, touchscreens, PC-era software) that happened to mature in parallel. However, BCI as a new environment requires a philosophical leap, not to mention a different stream of thought about what the downstream impacts could be (e.g. can the AI control the human through the BCI interface, will it be another voice inside the human's head?)

There are also many complexities to work through before this scenario can even be considered as plausible:

  • How might memory and context persist across many different interfaces of sensors, actuators or even lack thereof? (Google's cross-device announcement may indicate a solution for this first problem)
  • How can the intelligence identify and remember itself as the same entity rather than fresh and different instantiations of the intelligence?
  • Does this portability occur across all vendors or only within the same ecosystem? For instance, we see vendor lock-in even today with Apple's current marketing play. Could there be a world in which intelligence can move between different vendors and what would the implications be?

This isn't to say these ideas will become true, some science-fiction has a habit of remaining fiction even with time. But it does seem like all that is solid really does melt into air.
