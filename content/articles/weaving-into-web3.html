Weaving into Web3

Web 3.0: the next step in Internet history

 25 Oct '21

Last Tended: 30.10.21

Web 1.0 = Static Web

1998 - 2005 — User-interaction was fairly limited since at that point in time, the Internet was essentially a collection of digital documents. All you could was visit different websites and read the content there. Information merely traveled down a one-way street.

Web 2.0 = Social Web

2005 - Present — This was a period defined by the interactivity of likes, shares, follows. We have technologies like mobile and cloud, e-commerce platforms and social media networks. With companies as centralised platforms, they become intermediaries for our online transactions, exchanges and development of communities. Websites have also enabled user participation through interaction or development of Application Programming Interfaces (APIs). However, the sharing economy has also turned companies into mines of user information and they become targets vulnerable to failure. As middlemen channels, they're also privy to all the data flowing through it but we as users have little to no visibility as to what's happening under the hood. And we still play by their rules because we have no other option, right? Maybe.

Web 3.0 = Semantic Web

Present - Future — We're about to participate in another Internet evolution - the "Semantic Web". Web 3 is a relation between us and the tech. It has 4 features:

1. Decentralised systems

Blockchain is Web3's architecture. It connects all computers together and logs every transaction on a public electronic ledger. Its cryptographic timekeeping and verification through smart contracts (code that automatically executes when programmed) lets us check for ourselves if and when a transaction happened. Because of this transparency, you no longer really need to trust the other party at the end of the line, the network becomes trustless and decentralised. We don't need to give up our privacy to a middleman party. This network is built and maintained by users and participation can be incentivised with digital assets like Bitcoin.

Our professional world will change as well. Much like how Web 2.0 has created new jobs like digital marketer, Youtuber, influencer etc., the decentralisation that Web 3 brings will create a whole new suite of jobs. For instance, Decentralised Autonomous Organisations or DAOs. Instead of the more traditional, top-down hierarchical structures we have now, in a DAO anyone can join and is rewarded in tokens for the value they bring to a project. Likewise, Decentralised Applications (DApps) built on the blockchain allows users to truly control their own data.

Governance and regulation are evidently tricky things to tackle. How can we trust people will be of good faith? Perhaps accounts could get a score and based on the weighting access or rights would be granted (the person I was conversing with mentioned this idea was in a book). It’s an interesting premise, but one that’s possibly more temporal-based because what is trust but reliability i.e. consistent actions over time. If you're seen to contribute to the community, it's more likely you want to benefit the cause and not detract from it. Scoring accounts seems the case already with online communities like Reddit (using Karma) or StackOverflow (using Upvotes). The opinions of entrants don't have as much as weight as long-time members. However, in the instance of a decentralised community there may be other constraints because, arguably, we're not dealing just with comments but identities.

2. Artificial Intelligence

The data we generate allows us to co-create personalised solutions with the intelligence of the web. These include technologies like Big Data and AI which can aggregate enormous swathes of data. Siri, as an AI assistant who helps us fulfill tasks, is one example of a Web3 application hooked into the Web2 space. No doubt there will be further use cases as more products come online.

3. 3D graphics

The cyberworld will no longer stay 2D since graphics will improve to bring an even more immersive element to the real world - like avatars. Hence the current hubbub with Facebo- I mean, Meta’s current shift to the metaverse (a conversation for another time). Discussions of how beneficial virtual reality will be, and where it’s best placed in society, are once again brought to the forefront of collective consciousness.

4. Ubiquitousness

Computing will spread into everyday items other than our phone or computer, and will come online as part of the Internet of (every) Thing. Security concerns aside, a highly ubiquitous network enables us to interface with a whole range of objects and thus unlock capabilities beyond the material. Our world will become even more interconnected since the web uses and will deliberately seek out multiple information links to learn better.

(…frankly, these actually seem pretty handy)

Web3 is a common language we can use to not only talk directly to one another in a "peer to peer" manner, but we can also communicate with machines, and machines communicate with each other. Think of the Internet's evolution like the progression from a one way road, to a two way road and now we're interacting with the road itself and the cars around us. As with all technology there are pros and cons and feedback loops. Only time will tell as to whether this is a true zero-to-one movement in technological history. I, for one, believe it will be.
