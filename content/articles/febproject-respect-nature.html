Feb 2026 Project: Respecting Nature

initial experience of Nature

 21 Feb '26

We were a group of five/six snowboarders, aiming to finish the Hirafu Peak Gates at Annupuri Mountain via lift-access off-piste. Three of us were new-ish to snowboarding (okay enough to get down usual groomed runs) but also completely new to "backcountry" so didn't really know what it entailed. One of our friends was fine with leading us since he had completed the route before and had avalanche safety equipment.

Critical points during the trip:

  • Weather turned when we ascended - going from fine to 50-70km/h winds and poor visibility. The climb up became icy. Dex was with us for the first bit actually and had the good judgement to bail (people were surprised that I decided to continue once I saw her leave but didn't take this as a cue to quit as well). Going further up the mountain past that point was difficult to bail given the visibility.
  • I was struggling with my board on the hike up but refused help for a while. It felt like I was burdening them
  • While climbing down a ledge I slipped and fell, slid down the mountain for a bit. Felt scary and shaken but ultimately okay. We were very lucky two ski guides happened to be with us, one of which managed to arrest my slide.

After the run we had heaps of group and individual chats within the hiking group and wider circle. We realised a couple of people have differing opinions on safety/risk, what should or shouldn't have been done, what were good and bad decisions.

Reflections / questions:  - Safety awareness and asking questions - It didn't actually cross my mind to ask more questions about the route, and didn't realise it would be different to a groomed run. Also thought it would be fine because other people seemed chill with it (but that's probably just taking social cues from a crowd, not the best thing).

  • Being able to extrapolate tiredness / fatigue levels - Someone made a point that I should be able to know "if I feel this tired at this point, how tired might I feel later on?" How might I begin to build a familiarity or understanding with my normal fatigue levels?

  • Stubbornness and making a call on when to quit - Sometimes stubbornness as a trait can be helpful but not when it comes at a risk / danger. If I can't carry my own gear that's probably a good indicator that I should be turning back at some point.

  • Keeping composed in stressful situations?

Part of me realises it’s an exposure, experience and time thing, and we're currently working on the foundations of capacity.
