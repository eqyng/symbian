The future through lenses

Complex challenges require complex solutions

Last Tended: 17.07.21

The complexity of multifaceted world challenges will require equally complex solutions. Humanity does not yet have a clear vision of the future as these challenges take on fluid and historically-unprecedented forms.

This is how my application to a winter program in 2019 began (and before “unprecedented“ got associated with something else). I was trying to express how I saw aspects of society’s landscape being transformed by technology. I’ve been trying ever since. This is just another update:

*Technology x Psychology…*

Given society’s increasing digitalisation, technology probably forms the backbone of many people's thoughts. It’s common to the extent where the future looks a lot like Technology x [your profession]. But it’s not just in the professional sense, it’s also the personal. Everyone’s backgrounds come into the mix over the Internet, from personality to speaking style to humour and learned sensitivities. It’ll determine the way you build your online home, stake your plot in cyberspace, leave your footprints in the digital cement. It’s like feudal times, but the lands are infinite and made of opinions.

I’d say there have been long-held associations (read: my associations) of the digital being made of stark, cold binaries while humans are pliable, mushy molecules. But the conception that they’re totally removed from each other is being rewritten. Our complex relationship with technology, and the way it changes the relationship we have with each other and to ourselves, is a difficult one to articulate. Sometimes our intentions seem miraculously aligned, where technology improves humanity's quality of life, or at least doesn’t make it worse. At other times, they seem like tectonic plates pushing against each other with how debates have erupted - political, existential, pathological. We’re more aware that this relation isn’t as dichotomous as we’d previously thought. It’s more so that they’re two faces of the same coin - we have an effect on technology as much as technology has an effect on us. The reciprocal relationship between both is almost insidious in its subtlety, and the ubiquity of computers perhaps even more so.

The fields where biology and technology align are plenty, and so too are the issues associated with them. For instance, Artificial Intelligence. Since the selections of training data are being made by humans, it gives rise to concerns such as prejudice and discrimination, as developers unintentionally bake biases into their code (and the notion that it’s sometimes done intentionally can’t be escaped). On the whole, the products and services subsequently have an effect on us whether it’s short-term prompting of customer feedback or long-term psychological impacts. Other questions come with a philosophical bent - What does it mean to give robots consciousness, and what does it mean to augment our consciousness with robotics?

The idea of technological power is also clear. From established enterprises to nimble startups to the everyday individual, everyone wants a slice of digital pie. The thing is, once you purchase a smart device, you automatically have a ‘piece’. And you don’t need to be a tech enthusiast to do whatever you want with it. Such freedom is simultaneously liberating and restrictive. For the pedestrian consumer, we have a myriad of platforms on which we can express ourselves. But we also have a global audience, a record spanning lifetimes and digital vulnerabilities. Without fundamental digital knowledge, it’s relatively easy to be holding the wrong end of the knife, and yet not even realise it. For companies, it’s led to technological miracles that have had moonshot success (with corresponding financial success). But it may have also led to problems being retroactively shoehorned into solutions to justify their manufacture. So who is to take responsibility: The corporations for subliminally telling people what they want to line their pockets? The consumers for driving a capitalistic culture? The corporations for delivering a service that alleviates consumer problems? And around we go. Of course, there isn’t a singular Truth, but multiple opinions.

And this spares enough brain space to think about the future. It sometimes escapes me that younger generations, in all their rebellious, exploratory glory are continually architecting and reimagining the reality that we live in. A thought I have is whether we’re relying on them too much - these generations are old enough to put curiosity to use, but maybe too young to consider history as a factor when making (technological) decisions. Dystopian fiction is made of the ramifications of having few human values to guardrail design. I’m currently of two minds because 1) if we can’t transmit and anchor younger people in universal, stabilising values we could potentially have generations who are lost in more ways than one, but 2) maybe it’s just me, maybe it’s the values that need to change, perhaps this is how progress goes, and we just need trust them and in their ability to adapt (because they must).

*…x Design*

Although there are many other apertures through which the world can be interpreted, the one that strikes my eye is design. The interface is the only access point between technical jargon and the common user - otherwise everything is simply a functional, magical black box (well, phones are literal black boxes). Design, however, smooths this process, bringing selected aspects to the surface, highlighting some while hiding others. It has its merits in minimising information overload, and its loopholes.

Design also doesn’t merely mean the aesthetics of technology, but in all senses of the word. There’s the concrete aspects of interior design, community spaces and flexible living, as well as the more abstract aspects such as information architecture and language. If we follow the adage of “form follows function”, how will our high-speed, modern future be reflected in construction? And for a species who defines reality by five senses, what will the world be like if there’s more digital than physical substance?

*x Humanity*

Technology poses multiple unknowns, and everything feels like a double-edged sword. But the interrelated, transitory and dynamic nature of these systems is what appeals to me. It’s history in real-time, the only timestamps we’ll eventually have are through recordings, words and memories.

we must work together as a global society, bringing together different perspectives to gather more nuanced insights...By forming interdisciplinary teams, we will be able to find transformation in the intersection of seemingly disparate concepts.

2019 thought still holds. I’m by no means naïve about the chaos of humans. The exponentially growing complexity means we have many more creative interactions, and many more destructive blind spots. But I think the poison can also be the antidote. One way of managing the risks of the digital network could be through strengthening your surrounding human network. Others have suggested increasing the diversity of perspectives in the workplace or becoming more interdisciplinary. Things we’ve all heard before because the million dollar question (if dollars are measured in lives) still remains - How do we get there? What are the collective impacts on society's DNA? Like a blank clock face, I know that the seemingly simple response of “Just work together” is undergirded by complex, fragile cogs. Networked systems of trust and relationships painstakingly built over years can easily be disrupted, or even destroyed, within seconds.

So we go smaller - to the individual. “Working together” doesn’t necessarily mean we’re holding hands with radiant benevolent smiles. To me it means, among other things, co-creation with others through sometimes stressful situations, and the commitment to be mindful of your own influence whether that’s through your career, relationships or consideration of your temporal impact. Whatever’s within your realm of control. Because we’re all stewards of the future, and linked in ways we can’t imagine.

Maybe in the grand scheme of things my tech thoughts are just small issues. The letter, telegram, telephone, television, they all came and went. But similar old problems are present, just in new contexts, new forms. The interplay between both will reverberate through human history, as all transitions have. Speculations are nice and all, but the future is inherently uncertain. Like holding up a lantern in the deepest night, we can only see as far as five generations in front of us at a time.

Beyond that is the Unknown, and so the Now is up to us.
