Stewardship as AI Safety

on building across time

 12 Sep '26

What are you grateful for?

I think about being born at this exact point in time.

We are confronting a confluence of events and poly-crises. But there's something to be said about remaining ridiculously optimistic.

As I step further into AI safety and governance I find myself increasingly reaching for labels that describe my orientation.

There's a tension between two branches of my interests when it comes to AI safety and governance: risks and innovation.

Anticipating and understanding risks as well as interrogating assumptions is a non-negotiable for designing effective solutions. But I find pure remediation often becomes reactive, responding to impacts that may or may not eventuate. The work also doesn't always resolve the root issue because it occurs upstream. As we mature in safety practices across the board we are still learning to tune the level of documentation to its proportional risk.

Building systems and products that are widely-useful, improvable and scalable are some of the best ways to see humanity's resourcefulness. Our shared tenacity and ability to problem-solve is truly remarkable. But I'm skeptical of companies that grow for growth's sake (as Christian Heilman says "continuous growth is cancer"), solutions that prefer to keep accountability amorphous, and unconsidered tools that risk snapping delicate relational infrastructure developed over millennia.

It's useful that Risk and governance operate in an organisation as functions whose sole purpose is to ask the why alongside the what. But I'm finding it even more useful to define AI safety in terms of stewardship and anticipatory governance.

Why?

The concept of stewardship is governance that is not compliance, nor risk aversion. It is an act of care maintained over time such that future teams, users and generations are best positioned to sustain themselves. But also harnesses human potential so that everyone's current lives can be elevated without the cost to future lives.

Maintaining a steward view of resources means an attitude of preservation (of lessons, decision quality) such that future generations can continue the cycle of enablement and protection.

The position increases and enables collective capability while mitigating or reducing collective vulnerability and systemic harms. We must develop the ability to proactively design socio-technical systems that are resilient, safe and adaptable to unpredictable outcomes.

How?

Early thoughts have taken on the idea of "anticipatory governance".

Assurance-by-design focuses on proactively bringing risk reasoning upstream. Adaptability, experimentation and recovery are built into the DNA of complex systems so they are able to monitor and continuously learn from emergent outcomes. This supports humans within that system such that they retain the capability to notice issues, articulate why, and develop a solution.

A steward asks the necessary questions at all levels of a project:

  • Before
    • Who is this built for?
    • Should we build this? What evidence is there?
    • What assumptions are we making?
  • During
    • How do we later monitor it so it's functioning as intended?
    • How do we know it's working?
    • How can we intervene if it drifts over time?
  • After
    • What did we learn?
    • What did we find challenging?
    • How can we communicate this so that future teams can benefit?

What comes after is what I think is the most addressable at the moment in terms of current governance practice.

In some sense this position requires a level of detachment. But the idea that we're building for the future as a collective is where I find meaning and fulfilment. The fact that future generations will inherit decisions and systems they had no say in makes me highly conscious of the responsibility I hold now.

Nothing in this life is forever, only for now.

Are you a steward?
