March 2026 Project: Learn sign language

communicating differently

 25 Mar '26

I'd been wanting to learn sign language for a while.

Three things:

  1. As a fairly quiet child, one of the things people often asked me was to speak up, or speak louder, or turn to a person next to me and question whether I could speak at all.
  2. I had gone to a career's fair and noticed in passing that there was a deaf person signing animatedly to her conversation partner. I never forgot her facial expressions, and how joyful she looked.
  3. During my Psychology studies I learned that children who learned how to sign experienced less emotional meltdowns and frustration stemming from communication issues.

Learnings

  1. It was really cool to hear why other people came to the class. One person said they were going to go deaf in two years. A few people worked with children, another person
  2. I realised how dependent I was on hearing. In the first week there was an interpreter translating the teacher's signs for us. In deaf culture it's considered rude not to look at the deaf person signing, even if there is a person translating for them. It's equivalent to blocking your ears to someone speaking so to speak. But I found that I couldn't help looking at the translator. What my ears heard, my eyes immediately followed.
  3. The idea of "deaf gain" how being deaf is not a detractor, but something that adds to the culture around them.
  4. They are so expressive! And I have very limited facial expressions. An amusing memory is the teacher pointing out that we were learning how to sign "happy" or "sad" and yet we had a blank face for both.
  5. Their physical comedy is top tier.
  6. A memory I have was one of the teachers telling a story about how her dog passed away. It was possibly a 10 minute story but we shared oohs and ahhs.

It was an unfamiliar experience of a room being filled with silence and people and yet everyone being able to understand each other.

You needed to be patient, compassionate, attentive and truly want to understand what the other person was signing.
